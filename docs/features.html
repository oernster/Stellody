Features

Made for a collection
you spent years building.

The short version is here. Discovery, the library and playback each have a page of their own with the whole of it, in plain words; where something is not built yet, it is said there rather than left out and discovered later.

A wall of album covers

Browse by artwork or as a plain list, whichever suits you; one button switches between them. Covers come in three sizes. Click one and the album opens underneath without losing your place. Stellody remembers how you like it.

Find anything as you type

Start typing and your collection narrows instantly, however many thousands of songs it holds. The song you were looking for lights up so your eye goes straight to it, with the rest of its album still around it where you expect to see it.

Tune the sound to your ears

Ten sliders take the bass up or the treble down; you hear the change as you move them. A row of little bars dances along the bottom of the window showing what the music is doing. Turn the sliders off and Stellody adds nothing whatever to the sound.

Narrow it by genre

Genres come from a settled list of twenty-one, with thirty-six styles under them, so a tick means the same thing every time rather than depending on how a label happens to be spelled. Tick more and you see more. Albums that state no genre at all have a box of their own, so nothing is stranded behind a label nobody filled in.

Albums that flow, flow

Some records are made to run straight through with no pause between the songs. Stellody plays them that way, with no silence dropped in where the artist never put one.

The track exactly as the file holds it

One press hands the sound device the track exactly as the file holds it, with the system mixer out of the way. A line beside the time says what the device actually took, including "bit perfect" where that is true. Offered on Windows and macOS for any lossless song at a rate the device takes.

In detail

A page for each part.

Discovery

Stellody can use the collection you already own to find what you do not: albums by your artists that you never picked up, plus artists who sit close to the ones you love. Tick the genres worth looking in; it asks MusicBrainz and ListenBrainz, then lists what you do not hold.

The page covers what goes out, compilations, series and DJ mixes, years, how a search reports and stops, reading the answer and filtering it by kind and genre, then taking it to a shop.

Explore discovery

Your library

How Stellody reads the collection on your disk: scanning then rescanning only what changed, one folder to one album, box sets and cue sheets, muddled labels worked around without a file being touched.

Then how it shows it: a list or a wall of covers, search as you type, a settled list of genres, ratings and play counts, plus album art.

Explore your library

Playback & sound

How it plays: starting and stepping through tracks, shuffle and repeat, choosing the output device, albums that run straight through, the waveform and the buffering that keeps a busy computer from breaking up the sound.

What it does to the sound: a ten band equalizer that is untouched when off, the dancing bars, exclusive output and exactly when that is bit perfect and when it is not.

Living on your desktop

The window and the tray

It opens where you left it

Same size, same place, still maximised if that is how you left it. Unplug the big monitor and Stellody still opens somewhere you can actually reach it.

Maximise stays on your screen

On Windows with several monitors at different scaling, maximising from the title bar fills the screen that title bar is on, rather than whichever monitor Windows judged held most of the window.

It fits a laptop screen

Everything is drawn at nine tenths of its full size on every screen, so the whole window fits a 13 inch 4K laptop screen running at 300% scaling with nothing cut off at the edge. If you would rather have it larger or smaller, set QT_SCALE_FACTOR before starting it and Stellody uses your size instead of its own.

It can wait by the clock

Stellody can tuck itself down beside the clock and start there when you sign in, so it is ready without interrupting you. Opening it again brings your window back rather than starting a second copy.

The close button asks you once

The first time you close the window, Stellody asks whether that should tuck it away or shut it down properly. Tell it to remember and it never asks again. You can change your mind later.

A guide to the window itself

Help then Guide names every button on both trays beside the picture the window actually draws, so nothing has to be recognised from a description. Under that sit the four rules no single screen can state for itself: your files are only ever read, folders group while tags name, a correction differs from a stated tag, ratings follow the album rather than the file.

How to lay a library out

Stellody reads a collection the way it finds it, so the same guide states the rules it reads by: one album to a folder, discs of one release side by side, two folders joining where the artist and the title both agree, a cue sheet read only where the folder holds one audio file.

The short version is put in front of you before you choose a music folder for the first time, so you can go and look at what you have rather than find out afterwards.

Help, About and updates

The top tray ends with discovery, the light or dark appearance switch and Help. That one Help button drops the same menu as Help on the menu bar: the guide, the library health report, the two licences, About and Check for updates.

Every button is on a menu too

Anything a picture button does can be reached from the menu bar as well, the donation button alone excepted.

File repairs the library and discovers new music; Edit searches and filters; View switches between the sleeves and the list and picks a sleeve size; Sound switches exclusive output, chooses the output device, mutes, shuffles and chooses a repeat mode by name; Control plays, pauses, stops and steps between tracks. A tick beside an entry always says what is really on, however it was switched.
