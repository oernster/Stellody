Playing

Playing your music

Start it however you like

Double click a song, press Return, use the right click menu, the buttons across the top or an open album's play button. Stellody carries on through the album by itself.

You can always see what is playing

The playing song is marked on its row in the list and in the open album, even while another is selected. Its title and artists sit at the foot of the window while it is loaded: pausing keeps them, stopping clears them.

Click that name, choose Go to playing album on the View menu or press Ctrl+J: the album opens with the track picked out. Where a search or the genre filter hides it, the foot says so and the filter stays.

Back goes to the start first

Back returns to the start of the song you are on, waiting there for play. Press it again for the one before.

Volume, mute, shuffle and repeat

The right end of the bottom strip holds volume, mute, the output device, exclusive output and the equalizer, then shuffle and repeat. Volume, mute, shuffle and repeat are remembered. Repeat has three settings: off, the album and the one song.

Shuffle reorders the album afresh each time round. Every switch shows what pressing it would do, not what it is doing now.

A song that will not play says why

A drive not plugged in, a format this build cannot decode, a device another program holds exclusively: Stellody names the song and the reason at the bottom of the window, hands the sound device back and keeps the rest of your library working.

Choose where the music plays

A button on the bottom strip (also Output device on the Sound menu) lists System default then every device your computer can play through, two of one name numbered apart. Choose one and the song moves there; a paused song stays paused. The choice is remembered; the list keeps up as devices come and go.

Chosen headphones that disconnect pause the music; press play and it continues through the system default. Reconnect them and the music moves back by itself. A device that will not open leaves the music on the system default, with the reason shown. Tried on Windows, macOS and Linux with every listed device.

Headphones on, music follows

With System default chosen, headphones that become the system output take the song with them. Headphones switched off pause the music rather than switching to the speakers, with the reason shown; press play to carry on through the new output from where you were.

When Windows holds it silent

Windows keeps a volume and a mute for each program on each output device, in its Volume mixer. Held at 0 there, Stellody plays nothing while its visualiser still moves.

So when a song starts or resumes on Windows, Stellody reads its own entry. If Windows has it at 0 or muted, the foot of the window says so and where to change it. Stellody only reads that setting and never changes it; on macOS and Linux nothing is asked.

Albums that flow, flow

Live recordings, concept albums and most dance records run straight through; Stellody plays them with no silence added between the songs.

See the shape of the song

A picture of the whole track runs along the bottom with a line marking where you are, beside the time reached and the length. Click anywhere on it to jump there. A track played before draws at once; a new one is measured when highlighted, before you press play.

No static on a busy computer

Stellody keeps a generous reserve of sound queued at the output device, so a computer working flat out (a large build using every core, say) does not break the music up into static.

Measured and heard on Windows; macOS and Linux ask their mixer for the same reserve. Sound leaves the device a fraction of a second after decoding, which the position line and the visualiser allow for.

The sound itself

Tune it, then watch it move

Ten sliders, from deep bass to high treble

Open it from its button at the right of the bottom strip or from the Sound menu, then hear the change as you move each slider. A lift is given room rather than clipped: the whole curve is lowered by its largest lift, which keeps a boost from clipping on ordinary material, though not on every waveform.

So music plays quieter with a lift on; a curve that only cuts keeps its level. A separate on and off switch compares it with the untouched sound without losing your settings.

Switched off means genuinely untouched

Many players process your music even with everything at zero. Turned off, Stellody's equalizer hands each block back untouched rather than multiplying it by one; the volume at full does the same.

By default the result then passes through your system's mixer, which converts it, so it is not bit perfect. Exclusive output, below, bypasses the mixer.

Exclusive output, exactly as the file holds it

One press on the bottom strip asks the sound device for the music with the system mixer out of the way, the only way to be bit perfect. The song reopens where it was; a paused song stays paused. A line beside the time says what the device took: the mode, the rate, the depth and "bit perfect" where true. That also needs the volume at 100%, mute off and no equalizer curve (an equalizer on with every slider at zero applies none). The line updates as you move the volume, the mute or the sliders.

It is offered only for a song the device can take untouched: not a lossy song such as an MP3, nor one at a rate the device does not take, so a Bluetooth headphone taking 48 kHz alone cannot have a 44.1 kHz CD rip that way. The switch then shows the reason and the rates the device does take; your choice stands for the next song that can have it. With no song selected the device is judged against your library.

A new output device is asked afresh. One that refuses (because another application holds it, say) returns the switch to shared with the reason shown. On Windows the device is handed over outright; on macOS it runs at the song's own rate and refuses to convert, though other applications are still mixed in. Linux does not offer it.

A visualiser in ten styles

The visualiser in the middle of the bottom strip measures twenty bands, two to each of the ten sliders, so you can see which control owns the sound you hear.

A button just after the two view buttons lifts a list of ten styles, also Visualiser style on the View menu:

  • Bars
  • Meter
  • Wave
  • Flame
  • Mirror
  • Rainbow
  • Sunburst
  • Dot matrix
  • Scope
  • Bubbles

Every style draws the same twenty bands, so a style changes how the music looks, never what is measured. A new install starts on Dot matrix; an update keeps your choice.

Turning the volume down does not shrink it, since it shows the music rather than the volume. It measures after the equalizer, so with a lift on it mostly reads lower.

It cannot change what you hear

The visualiser watches the sound after it has gone to your speakers, so it cannot alter it. It needs no switch and stops when the music does.
